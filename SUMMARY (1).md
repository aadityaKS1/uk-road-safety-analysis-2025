# UK Road Traffic Collisions - 2025: Key Findings

*A one-page summary for non-technical readers*

## Bottom line

Late-night driving on high-speed single carriageway roads is the riskiest combination in the data. Safety spending should be prioritized there first.

## What we looked at

All reported road collisions in the UK during 2025 (STATS19 data), broken down by time of day, speed limit, lighting conditions, and road type.

## What we found

**1. Late night and early morning are the most dangerous times to be in a collision.**
A collision between roughly midnight and 5am is noticeably more likely to be serious or fatal than one during the morning commute (33% severe at 4am vs. 21% severe at 8am) - even though far fewer collisions happen at night overall. Fewer crashes, but each one carries more risk.

**2. Higher speed limits and darkness make a dangerous combination.**
Roads with a 60 mph limit have the highest rate of severe collisions of any speed category (36%). That risk climbs further when there's no street lighting - 60 mph roads with no lighting see close to 40% severe outcomes.

**3. Single carriageways are riskier than roundabouts, and the gap widens at higher speeds.**
On single carriageways (regular two-way roads with no central barrier), the severe-collision rate rises from 24% at 20 mph to 38% at 60 mph.

## What this means - and what it doesn't

These are patterns, not proof of cause. The data doesn't tell us how much traffic was actually on the road at each hour or location, so we can't say night driving *causes* worse crashes - only that it's associated with worse outcomes. Weather, traffic volume, and driver behavior weren't accounted for.

## Recommended action

Focus road-safety improvements - speed enforcement, better lighting, clearer signage - on high-speed single carriageway roads, especially locations with repeated severe collisions during the late-night/early-morning window. Before committing budget to a specific intervention, a follow-up look using actual traffic-volume data would sharpen the picture.
